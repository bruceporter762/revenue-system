# Market Signal Report — Jubilee Kits line extension (Graduation & Grandparent kits)

*Prepared by market-signal-researcher, using `business-brief.md` as input context. Covers two NOT-YET-BUILT product lines under a "Jubilee Kits" extension of By Celebration. No internal store data exists for these lines — everything below is external market research, scored by confidence.*

## Research questions

1. Is there validated seasonal/search demand for a "grab-and-go" graduation party kit, and for a grandparent-themed kit?
2. What do real competitors charge for comparable products, and does anyone already own the "one-stop bundle" positioning By Celebration uses for bachelorette kits?
3. What's actually resonating on TikTok/Pinterest/Instagram in each niche right now?
4. Does the existing "Ready-to-Go" bundle format (decor + favors + games in one SKU) fit the real demand shape for both lines, or only one?

## Findings

### Line 1 — Graduation kits

- **Seasonality (High confidence — NRF, Google-Trends-referencing blog sources):** Hard, well-documented spike. "Graduation party supplies" search interest peaks in May (~100 on a normalized 0-100 scale per trend commentary); "graduation party balloons" peaks similarly (~75). Most parties fall late May–mid-July, Saturdays most common. Photo-prop searches show a secondary rise in January (early planners) — worth an early-bird content angle.
- **Spend size (High confidence — National Retail Federation, 2026):** Consumers plan to spend an average of **$177 per graduate** in 2026; total U.S. graduation gift spend projected at **$7.2B**; 39% of consumers plan to celebrate a graduate this year (up from 36% in 2025). This is a real, growing, well-quantified seasonal spend pool — stronger hard-number backing than anything currently in `business-brief.md` for the existing bachelorette line.
- **Competitor pricing (Medium-High confidence — live listings found):**
  - Oriental Trading: room decorating kits **$11–13**; disposable tableware kits for 50 guests **~$28**; larger assortment/gift sets **~$33**.
  - Party City: sells tiered "kit for 16" / "kit for 24" party-supply bundles (plates, napkins, tablecover, banner, photo props) — exact current prices weren't retrievable from search results; **needs direct site check**, flagged low confidence on price point specifically.
  - Etsy: highly fragmented, $3.69 (digital download) to $49–82 (premium/customized boxes); a 4-box balloon+letters+banner set (66 balloons, "2026," "GRAD," "So Proud of You") is representative of mid-tier decor kits but exact price wasn't shown — medium confidence.
- **Platform content signals (Medium confidence — summarized from 2026 trend blogs/TikTok discover pages, not raw hashtag metrics):** Clean, color-blocked palettes (royal blue/white; blush/rose/ivory/gold), bow-and-pearl aesthetics, fillable grazing trays styled as "Pinterest-worthy," glittered custom signage, and fitted table covers are the recurring 2026 motifs. Content leans aspirational/aesthetic, which fits By Celebration's existing "playful, visually driven" brand voice well.
- **Gap identified:** No competitor found sells a single complete "everything-in-one-box" graduation kit (decor + favors + photo props + tableware) the way By Celebration's "Ready-to-Go" bachelorette kits do. Existing players are either narrow decor-only kits (Oriental Trading, Party City) or piecemeal favors (Etsy) — the exact fragmentation gap the brief's positioning already targets for bachelorette kits.

### Line 2 — Grandparent kits

- **Occasion date (High confidence):** National Grandparents Day 2026 = **Sunday, September 13** (first Sunday after Labor Day). This is a single-day observance, not a multi-week season — structurally a much narrower demand window than graduation's ~10-week spread or the bachelorette line's year-round demand.
- **Competitor pricing (Medium-High confidence):**
  - Big Dot of Happiness "Happy Grandparents Day" square favor gift boxes (set of 12) — **$15.99** on Walmart. This is a themed party-decor product, not a bundled kit.
  - Grandparent-themed gift baskets average **$80–$100** (per aggregated retailer data, e.g. Mrs. Fields-style baskets $86–96) — different product category (curated gift basket, not party kit).
  - Grandparent-focused subscription boxes start around **$14.58/box** — adjacent low-end price anchor, different (recurring) business model, low relevance to a one-time kit purchase.
- **Content/product-shape signal (Medium confidence):** Real demand signal for this occasion skews toward **personalized keepsakes** — photo puzzles, canvas prints, blankets, handprint art, "questions for grandma" books — clustering in the **$50–100** range, not toward party decor. Grandkids' activities skew toward shared experiences (baking, park visits) over "throw a party" behavior.
- **Gap identified:** No dedicated "one-stop Grandparents Day party kit" competitor found at scale. But — unlike graduation — this may reflect **genuinely different demand shape**, not just an unmet bundling opportunity: the market signal points to keepsake/gift format, not decor-bundle format. Cannot distinguish "gap = opportunity" from "gap = wrong product shape" using search data alone (see open questions below).

## Trade-offs and open questions

1. **Demand window size differs sharply between the two lines.** Graduation has a ~10-week selling season with strong quantified spend data; Grandparents Day is a single day. Annual revenue potential per line is not comparable — size both before committing equal inventory/marketing investment.
2. **Product-shape mismatch risk on grandparent kits.** Real signal points to keepsake/gift format (puzzles, canvases, blankets), not party-decor bundles. Forcing this into the same "Ready-to-Go" bachelorette-style bundle mold may miss actual demand. Flagging for offer-architect rather than deciding it here — that's offer-architect's call, not research's.
3. **Graduation market is more crowded and price-competitive** (Oriental Trading, Party City, and a large Etsy long tail all present) than By Celebration's current bachelorette-kit space. Differentiation has to be the complete-bundle positioning, since no one found currently owns it.
4. **Trend/search-volume data here is secondary-sourced** (blog commentary referencing trend data), not pulled directly from Google Trends' own interface — treat seasonality direction as reliable (multiple independent sources agree) but treat any specific index numbers as directional, not exact.
5. **Party City's exact current kit prices could not be confirmed** from search results — needs a direct site check before offer-architect benchmarks against it.
6. **Margins and COGS are unknown for both lines**, same open flag the brief already carries for the existing bachelorette kits — don't set pricing until real supplier/COGS numbers exist.
